# Seven-minute presentation

## 0:00–1:00 · State the product outcome

A complete English video becomes one complete lip-synced program per destination language. Ordered chunks are a processing strategy, not highlights. Read the 30-minute example: a five-minute cap creates six chunks and eighteen audio decisions for three languages.

## 1:00–2:00 · Show the source and provider boundaries

In Model inputs, compare duration-based and scene-informed partitioning. Source length remains variable; the cap is a different control. Scene boundaries here are synthetic. Inspect Normalize each chunk and ElevenLabs dubbed audio. ElevenLabs owns translation/audio dubbing; the app does not duplicate its internal model stack.

## 2:00–3:15 · Listen and approve audio

Open Audio approval. Explain the reference fixture label. Play the sample, enter a demo reviewer name and confirm the three checks. Approve one audio package. Show that the current version/hash is bound to the decision. Alternatively, request a new recording or reject it. Real locally loaded audio can be reviewed without uploading it.

## 3:15–4:15 · Follow Sync and the batch join

Run or advance simulated time. Sync starts only with a matching approval and uses separate video/audio inputs, without dubParams. Inspect a partial batch in Final review; missing chunks prevent a complete assembly. For a quick demonstration, start with a one-chunk source/language package.

## 4:15–5:15 · Perform the second human review

After all chunks finish, Final review presents the assembled program. The bundled video is a clearly labeled waveform fixture, not a Sync face result. Play it, demonstrate the final checks and explicitly approve or hold it. Only this second approval allows delivery. A real final video may also be loaded locally.

## 5:15–6:00 · Inspect failure and decision evidence

Use Package traces and filter to a delivered program. Its spans include source segmentation, chunk history, audio approval, Sync, reassembly and final approval. Show the decision trail and source interval coverage. Rejected audio cannot create an accepted partial program.

## 6:00–7:00 · Explain operations and economics

Show independent provider quotas, application workers and planned human capacity. Model economics separates actual simulated spend from analytical expectations. Rates are editable assumptions; documented Sync references are plan- and frame-rate-dependent. Expected acceptance percentages never approve a live job. End with the largest production validation needs: actual provider behavior, authentic reviewer identity, durable manifests/decisions and representative quality benchmarks.
